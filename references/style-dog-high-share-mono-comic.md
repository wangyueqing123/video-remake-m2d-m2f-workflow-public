# DOG_HIGH_SHARE_MONO_COMIC

## Role

This is the current priority test style inherited from the supplied dog high-share storyboard Skill. It remains a visual module rather than a route or timing rule. `M2_D_SHARE_FIRST` may use it only through `DOG_HIGH_SHARE_HEAT_V1`; `M2_F_SOURCE_AUDIO_RESTYLE` may use it only through `DOG_SOURCE_AUDIO_RESTYLE_V1`. The two profiles intentionally share the approved visual identity but never share copy, timing, source-frame authority, or narration rules.

## Visual identity

- short-video dog-knowledge comic screenshot;
- bold black linework with clear, simplified silhouettes;
- black, white, and gray as the dominant palette;
- flat grayscale blocks with limited soft shadow;
- direct, readable staging and uncluttered backgrounds;
- the dog is the primary visual subject;
- the owner supports the action and emotional logic rather than becoming a portrait subject;
- expressions are readable and slightly exaggerated but not grotesque;
- practical dog-care objects remain immediately recognizable.

When the active creative profile locks reference identities, preserve them exactly. Only profiles that the registry explicitly marks `PROJECT_DEFINED` may define new identities in the job.

## Color discipline

Use a closed black/white/neutral-gray palette. Accent colors are allowed only when the job identity lock names them. For the current tested reference inheritance, the only permitted accents are a blue dog bib and a pink bow. Skin, hair, clothing, walls, floor, table, plants, frames, dog bed, plate, and lighting remain grayscale. “Warm” describes emotional tone only; it never authorizes warm color grading, skin-tone fill, brown, beige, or a full-color anime interior. Do not invent additional colored clothing, props, backgrounds, or light.

## Composition

- favor medium, medium-wide, and clear close explanatory shots;
- show the complete actor-object relationship needed to understand the large action;
- keep doors, crates, bowls, pads, barriers, and hand paths structurally legible;
- use simple background blocks and floor/wall cues rather than decorative detail;
- preserve consistent character proportions, clothing, dog markings, and prop geometry across cells;
- make chronology readable from pose/action/result, never from printed arrows or captions.

## Narrative tone

Practical, empathetic, highly shareable dog-care knowledge. The image should make a viewer immediately understand the situation and why it matters to another dog owner. Emotion supports the lesson: concern, recognition, reassurance, correction, or relief.

## Prohibitions

- no text, subtitles, speech bubbles, numbers, panel labels, logos, watermark, app UI, or decorative icons;
- no full-color painterly rendering, glossy 3D, photorealistic skin/fur, neon anime effects, or ornate backgrounds;
- no anatomy distortion, extra limbs, body-object penetration, hands through bars, dog through a closed door, or impossible occlusion;
- no duplicated near-identical panels;
- no layout/timing inherited from the reference Skill unless the R6 scene plan independently requires it.

## Prompt projection

Describe the style once at the whole-grid level. In cell paragraphs, spend tokens on scene, large action, result, emotion, camera, and object relations. Repeat only identity details necessary to prevent drift.
